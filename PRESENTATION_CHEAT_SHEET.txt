█ LIKELY QUESTIONS & SHORT ANSWERS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Q: "Why not deep learning?"
A: "Random Forest achieved R²=0.89, which is excellent. Deep learning would add
    complexity without clear benefit. Also, RF is much easier to interpret."

Q: "How do you know it's causal?"
A: "Two ways: 1) We controlled for major confounders, 2) We used Propensity 
    Score Matching which mimics an experiment. Effect persists after matching."

Q: "Why is fertility so important?"
A: "High fertility means: resource dilution, closely spaced births stress 
    maternal health, correlates with lower education. It's a proxy for a 
    whole cluster of socio-economic conditions."

Q: "If socio-economic factors are 92%, why care about pollution?"
A: "Two reasons: 1) 8% of global child mortality is still millions of deaths,
    2) Pollution is more actionable - can be reduced in years vs decades for 
    economic development."

Q: "What about other pollutants?"
A: "Future work. We focused on PM2.5 because it's most harmful to children -
    fine particles penetrate deep into lungs. Challenge is data availability."
